Hands Against the Wall

Move towards the candle and the shadow grows, and softens at the edges, exactly as much as it should.

The hand

The room

 

The shadow is projected, not drawn bigger. The flame is a point in the room at a known distance from the wall; your hand is somewhere between them. Every point of the hand is cast along the straight line from the flame through it and onto the wall, which works out as a magnification of D / (D − d) about the flame’s position — where D is the flame’s distance from the wall and d is your hand’s. Right against the wall the shadow is life-size. Halfway, it is twice the size. Nine tenths of the way, ten times. That is why moving your hand a few centimetres near the candle throws a wolf across the whole room.

The soft edge is real too. A flame is not a point, it is a small bright thing, so every edge has a penumbra whose width grows with the same ratio — and that is why a shadow held close to the wall has a hard edge and one held near the candle is a vague dark cloud. Both the blur and the growth come off the same two numbers here; nothing is faked to look right.

The eight hands are drawn here, as silhouettes, from the shapes everybody’s hands eventually find. Nothing is photographed and nothing is traced.
