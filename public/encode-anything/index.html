Encode Anything

binary · hex · base64 · morse · flags
Everything is converted in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere. Text is treated as UTF-8, so a character outside ASCII becomes several bytes and the binary and hex show all of them; base64 is the standard alphabet with padding. Morse has no case, no punctuation beyond a few marks, and no letters outside A–Z and 0–9, so anything else is dropped rather than invented — the count above says how much. Semaphore is the two-flag alphabet, and it is generated from the rule rather than typed out: eight arm positions, seven “circles”, one flag holding still while the other walks round. That construction produces exactly 26 letters and the two service signs, which is why J sits where it does rather than after I — and it means the table cannot contain a typo.
