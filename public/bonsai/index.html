A Tree That Takes Its Time

It grows by the calendar, not by the visit. Come back tomorrow and there will be a little more of it than there is now.

Nothing here happens because you are watching. The tree’s whole shape is settled the day it is planted — every branch it will ever have, and the day each one opens. What the calendar does is decide how much of that shape has happened yet. Staring at it does nothing; leaving it alone for a fortnight does a great deal.

Pruning is not damage, it is direction. Cutting a branch takes it and everything above it away for good, and in exchange the rest of the tree gets a day’s worth of vigour it would not otherwise have had. That is roughly what happens in a real pot: take the leader off and the tree spends what it was spending there somewhere else. A tree left entirely alone will still fill out; it will just look like every other tree left alone.

The leaves follow the real month where you are — green through the summer, turning in the autumn, bare in winter, and the buds back in spring. There is no watering, no wilting and nothing to fail. It is kept in this browser, so it is yours and only yours, and it is still growing while the tab is shut.
