Ondaric

The whole grammar

  1. The sentence runs backwards. Ondaric is head-final throughout, so what English puts first, Ondaric puts last.
  2. Every letter has one counterpart. Vowels become vowels and consonants become consonants, so a word keeps its shape and stays sayable.
  3. An article binds to the front of its noun with a turned comma: sa’noun.
  4. A preposition becomes a postposition and binds to the back with a hyphen: noun-esh. Both can happen at once.
  5. Punctuation becomes a particle word, written with a raised dot so it can never be mistaken for a root.
  6. A capital letter stays a capital.

Ondaric was built backwards from one requirement: whatever goes in has to come out again. Every rule above is reversible, and the pair of them round-trips four thousand randomly generated sentences without losing a word. The shared word reshuffles both the letters and the particles, so two people who agree on a word have a language that a third person cannot read even with this page open in front of them — but it is a party trick, not cryptography, and a determined reader with a page of your text and an afternoon will get it. Do not use it for anything that matters.
