The Chladni Plate

Sand finds the parts that are not moving

—

The pattern is not decoration. Drive a plate at one of its resonant frequencies and parts of it stand still while everything around them moves; sand skitters off the moving parts and piles up along the still ones, so the nodal lines draw themselves. Ernst Chladni was doing this with a violin bow in the 1780s.

These are the nodal lines of an ideal square plate, from the standard model cos nπx cos mπy − cos mπx cos nπy, and the frequency is scaled from the mode numbers so the slider covers an audible range. A real plate of a particular size, thickness and metal has its own set of frequencies, and they are not these — the shapes are the honest part, the numbers are a stand-in.
